# 2048 controller qualification

The original JereTheJuggler project is packaged as `2048-JereTheJuggler.sb2`,
SHA-256 `ff11d9adfa7fb1b38cdcef5711132d060dba96d91d371853e854456f00b0607f`.
Its installed sidecar maps both D-pad and left stick to arrows, and A to Space.
The original has no authored sound-playing opcodes.

## Grid/input investigation

The earlier persistent board at Score 14448 can be explained by two legal Up
moves, including their random spawns. That explanation is an inference from
board and score observations; the original event did not include an input-edge
trace. Two previous xemu seed tests each passed 1,000 iterations of the original
Up/spawn procedures. These tests do not prove the cause of an extra hardware
move. See the [earlier score audit](xbox-game-qualification.md#2048-long-run-and-score-audit).

A new xemu comparison used the unmodified project, its ordinary title/start
path and controller input. Each of four 1.8-second direction holds (Up, Left,
Down, Right) produced exactly one legal move and one new tile, with the expected
score gain. GDB only read the board; the debug startup skipped the emulator's
network wait and selected the initial DVD path. This bounded comparison does
not establish that the intermittent hardware event is fixed.

The opt-in `SE_XBOX_TRACE_MENU_INPUT` build now records up to 512 button/key/mouse
state transitions after each browser load or pause action, in addition to the
eight existing post-handoff polls. The normal build compiles out these traces.
The trace is observational: no input filter, repeat policy or grid code changes.

One commanded 300 ms Down therefore appeared as two presses. The board and

## Victory acknowledgement

The original `you win` receiver waits only for `mousePressed`. On the Xbox,
A maps to Space, so A cannot acknowledge that panel and resume the move loop.
Prepare a separate controller-friendly copy with:

```sh
python3 scripts/prepare-2048.py ORIGINAL.sb2 PREPARED.sb2
```

The prepared copy changes that one wait to accept mouse **or Space**, retaining
mouse support. It leaves the grid, score, random spawn, loss/replay scripts,
artwork and credits unchanged. All 30 assets are byte-identical. Reversing the
one expression restores exactly the original project JSON. Original/output
overwrite rejection, alternate-source rejection, reproducible output and Python
compilation were checked.

Prepared SHA-256:
`088e8fdc2bc4339ca3f0244b149cb151d713906533decba209f65624e83c78e0`.
Keep the existing sidecar beside the deployed filename.

The xemu victory test uses a **separate diagnostic seed**, not a normal
playthrough: an added delayed driver places two 1024 tiles and resets score
after the original start scripts. A normal Left controller move then executes
the original merge, scoring and win scripts. On the original copy, A followed
by Down left the panel at ghost 20 and the board unchanged. On the prepared
copy, A dismissed the panel (ghost 100), and a subsequent
Down moved the 2048 tile and added one new tile. Score remained 2048. Both
copies used the same seed and engine; the seed driver is absent from the
distributable prepared archive.

Ignored local evidence is under `build-xbox/xemu-2048-input/`: matching XBE and
symbol hashes, the four `after-one-*-hold.json` snapshots,
`held-input-verification.json`, `win-seed-manifest.json`,
`win-original-after-A.json`, `win-prepared-before-A.json`,
`win-prepared-after-A-and-Down.json`, and `preparation-validation.json`.
Fixture tests are separate from controller-only hardware play and do not establish a full
2048 hardware win.

## Unseeded normal-build hardware victory — October 27, 2026

The canonical prepared archive completed an ordinary controller-played round on
installed diagnostics-off XBE `2b8b5778…`. The browser launched its regular
title; A started a fresh Score 0 round with a 2 and a 4. No fixture, debugger,
seed, or project-state writes were used on the physical Xbox.

Two consecutive controller segments sent 900 and 27 direction commands without
resetting the round. Fresh HDMI board observations were compared with the
original move/merge rules and legal random spawns. Twelve commands produced no
observed change. One transition is compatible with two legal Left moves and
spawns; it was recorded and play continued from the observed board. There is
no console input trace for that event, so it does not independently isolate the
extra move's cause. Every other changed board matched one legal commanded move
and one spawn. The final board after acknowledgement also matches the final
merge plus one spawn.

The final Left merged two 1024 tiles and two 2 tiles, adding 2052 points. The
screen displayed `You Win!` at Score 20264, exactly matching the independent
sum of legal merge gains from the initial zero. A dismissed the panel and
revealed the ordinary rainbow 2048 tile. Continued Down and Right moves added
4 and 8 points, respectively, reaching the visibly confirmed Score 20276.
Right moved the 2048 tile from the bottom-left cell to the third column. The
other fifteen cells were pixel-decoded; that animated rainbow tile was checked
visually after its moving colors exceeded the static palette matcher.

The runtime pause menu's Green Flag returned to the ordinary title. A started
another Score 0, two-tile round; Up followed by Left produced a normal merge
and Score 4. Exit returned to the populated browser, and a fresh browser launch
repeated the title and an ordinary zero-score start.

On that fresh launch, 64 further direction commands produced a natural Game
Over at Score 388. The independent final move/spawn calculation agrees with
the visible score, and the visually read full board has no legal move in any
of the four directions. A reset it to Score 0 with two fresh tiles; Up moved
them and spawned a new tile. A second short-confirmation Exit returned to the populated browser. Both
returns used 200ms A confirmations; they do not resolve the separate held-A
handoff failure. These checks used the same installed normal XBE.

A twelve-second, 48kHz stereo HDMI capture during this round contains 576,000
frames of exact digital silence. This agrees with the project's absence of
authored sound playback and confirms no residual Mario audio in that sample.
It does not resolve the shared KVM delivery/handoff issue.
